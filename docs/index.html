Petrol and diesel prices push inflation to highest in a year

UK inflation reached its highest in a year at 3.6% in October, driven by increased petrol and diesel prices and elevated oil costs.

Official figures from the Office for National Statistics (ONS) show that the UK's inflation rate rose to 3.6% in the year to October, up from 3.5% in September, primarily due to higher petrol and diesel prices. Elevated oil prices also impacted the wider economy, increasing raw material and factory gate costs. Inflation is expected to climb further, with gas and electricity bills forecast to jump by 26% in January, adding £443 to annual household energy costs. The Bank of England raised interest rates from 3.75% to 4% earlier this month, the first time in three years, to curb inflation, and signalled further rises if the US-Iran conflict is not resolved. Emma Reynolds, chief secretary to the Treasury, stated the government is providing support, including a VAT cut on electricity bills to zero until next July and a £100 payment to 20 million homes in January.

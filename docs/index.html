Binance says fake job interviews used as part of $6.3bn crypto hack

Hackers stole a record $6.3 billion from Binance using fake job interviews and a compromised internal AI tool, leading to a temporary suspension of withdrawals.

Hackers orchestrated a "sophisticated, multi-stage campaign" to steal a record $6.3 billion (£4.7 billion) from Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange. The attack involved social engineering techniques, where an engineer was contacted through a legitimate job-hunting platform and invited to bogus job interviews. During a second interview, the engineer was instructed to download malware disguised as an "assignment," which deployed malicious code. The perpetrators used "more advanced and deceptive" methods, including sharing fake social media accounts, to build trust. Hackers then leveraged the compromised computer and an internal AI tool to gain control over two other engineers' laptops, executing further attack steps. This incident led Binance to suspend global withdrawals, causing concern and disruption for users. Binance has since set aside over 100% of the funds to cover the loss and replenished 98% of impacted token assets, while working with law enforcement.

Rủi ro
The use of sophisticated social engineering and AI tools by hackers poses a significant and evolving cybersecurity risk for financial institutions and technology companies.

Further details from Binance's investigation, the effectiveness of their enhanced security measures, and the broader implications for cybersecurity practices in the crypto industry.
Hàm ý
This record-breaking hack underscores the critical need for robust cybersecurity protocols, employee training against social engineering, and secure management of internal tools, especially AI, within the cryptocurrency and tech sectors to prevent massive financial losses and maintain user confidence.

8/10

This move is set against a backdrop of increasing geopolitical tensions and a global push for technological sovereignty, where nations are keen to protect critical technologies and data. The chain of impact is significant: it will likely make it more challenging for Chinese companies to acquire foreign tech firms, particularly in sensitive sectors like AI, and could deter foreign companies from seeking to acquire Chinese assets if regulatory hurdles become too high. This policy shift reflects a broader trend of de-globalization in certain strategic sectors and an emphasis on national security over purely economic considerations. Historically, governments have often intervened in cross-border M&A for national security reasons, but the current intensity from Beijing signals a more systemic approach. The positive aspect, from China's perspective, is enhanced national security and control over strategic industries. However, the risks include reduced global M&A activity involving Chinese entities, potential retaliatory measures from other countries, and a chilling effect on innovation if companies become too cautious about international collaboration.

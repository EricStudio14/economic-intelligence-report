8/10

The U.S. dollar hitting 13-month highs is a direct consequence of investors betting on continued interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve, a sentiment reinforced by recent hawkish signals from the Fed. This development occurs in a global economic environment where inflation remains a persistent concern, prompting central banks, particularly the Fed, to prioritize monetary tightening. Higher interest rates in the U.S. make dollar-denominated assets, such as Treasury bonds, more attractive to international investors, thereby increasing demand for the dollar. This strengthens the dollar against other major currencies. The positive aspect is that a strong dollar can help curb imported inflation for the U.S. and reflect confidence in the U.S. economy. However, the risks are significant: a strong dollar makes U.S. exports more expensive, potentially hurting American companies, and it puts immense pressure on emerging markets that have dollar-denominated debt, making repayment more costly. This situation also impacts commodity prices, as many are priced in dollars, making them more expensive for non-dollar holders.

How a sex scandal at Nvidia is putting pressure on the White House

Allegations of an affair involving Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and a former Megaspeed executive, suspected of Chinese intelligence links, are pressuring the White House over US technology export controls and China policy.

Allegations surrounding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's reported affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, have raised concerns about a potential foreign intelligence operation targeting a powerful businessman with close access to the American president. US intelligence reportedly suspects Alice Huang of having ties to Chinese intelligence, though Beijing denies these claims and Nvidia's board states no company secrets or national security were compromised. This situation has brought scrutiny upon Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whose department controls sensitive US technology exports and was pressed to act on concerns about Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer suspected of diverting advanced chips to China. Megaspeed CEO Tan Yong Pong (James Tan) affirmed the company's commitment to compliance and cooperation with the US government. A highly classified memo about Alice Huang and Megaspeed was shared with Lutnick, who resisted taking action against the company, a decision now under review. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and other senior officials questioned why Megaspeed was not blacklisted. An investigation into Megaspeed was reportedly halted after Nvidia's general counsel intervened, invoking a senior Trump appointee, leading to the case being transferred to the Justice Department. This incident highlights a significant disagreement within the Trump administration regarding China policy, with Huang seeking Chinese market access while others worried about national security implications, leaving Lutnick in a difficult position.

A Microsoft outage, which has affected services such as Outlook, has persisted for over 20 hours, a duration considered exceptionally long for such disruptions. Prof James Ferryman from the University of Reading's department of computer science explained that the issue stems from the part of Microsoft's servers responsible for secure connections becoming overwhelmed, causing them to stop responding. When one server fails, its workload shifts to others, while a massive number of devices continuously attempt to reconnect, making a simultaneous restart impossible. Gergely Orosz, a former leading software engineer at Skype and Uber, described this as potentially the "most disruptive" business email outage "of all time," noting he cannot recall a similar disruption lasting this long. Australian cyber-security expert Troy Hunt highlighted the far-reaching consequences, emphasizing Microsoft's role in the internet backbone and email's criticality for work, appointments, purchases, and account access.
